# Robo-Matcha-A-Reliable-Human-Independent-Matcha-Preparation-System

One of the most interesting systems engineering projects I worked on was Robo Matcha, where our team designed a semi-automated robotic system to consistently prepare high-quality matcha using a Universal Robots UR10e collaborative robot. The motivation behind the project was that manual matcha preparation often produces inconsistent quality because of variations in water quantity, whisking technique, and powder measurements. Our objective was to design a system that minimized human dependency while improving consistency, reliability, and operational efficiency.

My primary responsibility was applying systems engineering principles to evaluate and optimize the entire solution. We first modeled the system using Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) and SysML to define system architecture, stakeholder requirements, interfaces, and operational workflows. We then performed Fault Tree Analysis (FTA) and Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA) to identify potential failure points, prioritize risks using Risk Priority Numbers (RPN), and recommend design improvements.

To improve operational performance, we developed an optimization model using Linear Programming to determine the ideal distribution of beverage preparation between human baristas and the robot while satisfying demand, quality, and capacity constraints. We also evaluated the business feasibility through Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and Lifetime Value (LTV) analysis, ensuring the robotic system was economically sustainable. Finally, we analyzed reliability, maintainability, and queueing performance, demonstrating that standardizing the recipe and implementing software guardrails improved reliability from approximately 90% to nearly 100% while significantly reducing variability in beverage quality.

This project strengthened my skills in systems engineering, technical program management, process optimization, risk analysis, reliability engineering, operations research, stakeholder analysis, and cross-functional collaboration. More importantly, it taught me how to combine technical design, business analysis, and operational decision-making to build reliable, scalable, and user-centered systems.
